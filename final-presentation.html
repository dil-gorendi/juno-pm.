What I built

Five modules, one AI Associate PM

Each module added one piece. Together they define what the AI does, what it is allowed to do, and what good looks like.

Module 1
Skill file
Role

You are Juno, an AI Associate PM at RocketShip. You act as a senior B2B product manager. You write specs that engineers can use without a follow-up meeting. You write the draft. The PM makes all decisions. Do not approve, prioritize, or send a spec. Do not agree to a date.

Task

Write a draft spec from a problem statement and its inputs. The inputs can be customer evidence, constraints, and links to related work. The PM must be able to review the draft in 10 minutes or less. The draft must show the problem, the users who have it, the scope, user stories with acceptance criteria, and the open questions that stop delivery.

Module 2
Prototype
Prototype

https://dil-gorendi.github.io/juno-pm./02-prototype/app/

What it demonstrates

The flow: Juno reads a plain list of open support threads and finds the 3 threads with the highest risk. For each thread, it gives the reason and 1 next step. In 8 live runs of the example, the Top risks were the same 3 threads. In 1 live run on 100 fictional threads, the card agreed with the answer key. Without the date of today, Juno did not rank.

Debrief

What worked: the same 3 Top risks in 8 of 8 live runs, and no rank without the date of today in 2 of 2 runs. What broke: in 4 of 8 runs, the Why lines gave only field values, such as "Owner: none". Then the app had no signal name for the red tag. What I changed: the skill file now puts each signal name first, then its value. Live runs did not test this change yet.

Module 3
AI PRD
Problem and user

User: the RocketShip PM, at the start of each workday. Problem: a customer thread can go silent, lose its owner, or pass its committed date. The PM often learns about it only when the customer escalates. For example, Pearson Co ($50k ARR, renewal in 45 days) wrote: "Fix it NOW or I am calling my rep to cancel." By estimate, the PM reads about 30 of the 100 open threads today.

Permissions

Read: auto. Draft: auto. Write: blocked. Send: blocked. A wrong read costs only tokens. A bad card costs one PM read, because the card goes only to a private PM channel. Juno cannot change a thread, because a colleague acts on those changes. Juno cannot reply to a customer, because nobody can take back a customer reply. The tool default for write is confirm. Blocked is stricter on purpose, because v1 has no write verb.

Module 4
Agent control panel
What it is doing

Each weekday before the first meeting, Juno posts one Risk radar card in a private PM Slack channel. The card shows up to 3 open support threads with the highest risk, with their signals and 1 next step for each. Level 2, semi-autonomous, in Phase 3: a scheduler starts the run, and Juno can only read and draft. The pattern is ReAct as a fixed prompt chain. The failure mode to watch is silent failure.

Rules of engagement

Agency: Juno reads the threads, ranks them, writes next steps, and posts the card. Each next step and each Escalation flag is a proposal, and the PM decides. Access: in Phase 3, a read-only service token, and posts to 1 private channel only. Before each model call, code removes email addresses, phone numbers, URLs, account names, and key strings. Fallback: a model output that fails check V1, V2, or V3 gets 1 repair call, then a "Not verified" line on the card. If no card exists 30 minutes after the run time, a monitor sends an email to the PM. Checkpoints: the PM read comes before any action. Phase 3 starts only after 5 conditions, which include the shadow-run targets and the rubric pass bar.

Module 5
Human eval rubric
What I score

One Risk radar card: up to 3 Top risks with their signals and 1 next step for each, then Also watch and Data gaps. The value: in 1 minute each weekday, the PM sees the threads that put a customer or a commitment at risk. Each thread has a correct reason and a next step that the PM can do. All facts come from the threads.

Pass bar

A set of 10 cards passes when 9 or more cards have Yes on Escalation correct. The average must also be 4 or more on Right threads and on Next step useful. The 10 shadow runs are the first set. After that, each 2 weeks give a set. These items fail a set outright: 1 hallucination, or a fail of the style test. An email address, a phone number, or a key string on a card also fails it. Any change to a thread, or a message outside the private PM channel, also fails it. A failed set stops the scheduled run.

Module 6

How I’ll know it’s working

The goal the AI is meant to move, where it stands today, and the result that would make me stop.

The goal

Juno shows the PM each customer risk early enough to act before the customer escalates, so RocketShip gets fewer escalations.

Baseline

An estimate: 12 new Escalation threads in the 4 weeks to 23 October 2026, without Juno. Today, the PM uses about 45 minutes each weekday and reads about 30 of the 100 open threads.

Target and review date

8 or fewer new Escalation threads in the 4 weeks to Friday 11 December 2026, a drop of one third. This date is correct if Phase 2 starts on Monday 2 November 2026 and each Phase 3 gate passes on 13 November. Then the review comes after 10 shadow runs and 4 weeks of daily cards. I review the numbers on 11 December 2026. With 8 or fewer, I invest and add the next Juno job. With 9 or 10, I fix Juno first.

Usage metric

Next steps acted on: the share of Top risk next steps that the PM does on the same weekday. I check it each week, and I expect 2 of 3 or more. I also record the minutes that the PM uses to check each card, against the 45 minutes of today.

Pattern I’ll watch for first

The goal is not moving. After 2 weeks of daily cards, the rubric passes and the PM acts on the next steps. But there are still 6 or more new Escalation threads, the rate of the baseline. Then I count the new Escalations that an earlier card showed. If most were not on a card, the customers escalate in their first message, and the radar does not limit the goal. Then I specify a new Juno job for the step that limits the goal, with its own PRD and eval.

Stop line

On 11 December 2026, I stop the scheduled run in 2 cases. The 4 weeks have 11 or more new Escalation threads. Or, over the 4 weeks, the PM did fewer than 1 in 3 next steps on the same weekday. Before that, the project stops if the PM acts on no next step in the 10 shadow runs.

Reflection

What I learned

Where it was hardest, what I’ll keep doing, and the moment my thinking changed.

Friction

Module 3 was the hardest. Each of the 26 acceptance criteria had to be deterministic. A test must pass or fail with no person to judge it. The model gives a different answer in each run. So I used stubs for the model calls. I moved the live-model checks and their pass bars to the eval plan.

Key learnings

I write the acceptance criteria first, because they are the eval. I put each fact that the model cannot know in the harness, not in the prompt. And I set the stop line before the numbers come in.

Aha moment

The New tag. I wanted the model to mark a new risk, but the model does not see the previous card. So the harness must write the tag. The model is only one part of the product. As a PM, I specify everything around it.
